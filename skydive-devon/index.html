Why skydive in Devon?

Dunkeswell is one of the prettiest places in the country to jump. On a clear day the view from altitude stretches from Weymouth to Wales, taking in both the north and south coastlines.

Jumps leave from a turbine-powered aircraft, the Cessna 208B Grand Caravan, which climbs quickly and smoothly. The scenic flight to altitude takes roughly 15 to 20 minutes, giving you time to take in the Devon and Somerset countryside before the door opens. The atmosphere on the ground is relaxed and local rather than a conveyor belt, and a one-to-one tandem skydive means Rich is with you from your first message to your debrief. What happens on the day of your skydive

  1. 1

    Arrive and sign in

    You check in at Dunkeswell Airfield and complete your paperwork.

  2. 2

    One-to-one safety briefing

    Rich explains body position, the exit from the aircraft and what to expect, at your pace.

  3. 3

    Kit up

    You are fitted with a jumpsuit, harness and goggles, and do a short pre-jump video interview.

  4. 4

    Final checks and the climb

    After the flight line checks you board the aircraft for a scenic climb of roughly 15 to 20 minutes over the Devon and Somerset countryside.

  5. 5

    Freefall

    Roughly 30 seconds from 10,000ft or up to about 60 seconds from 15,000ft. It is loud, so Rich uses hand signals.

  6. 6

    Under canopy

    Your parachute opens by 5,000ft and it goes quiet. You can enjoy the view, try some turns, and if there is time and it is safe, steer the canopy yourself.

  7. 7

    Landing and debrief

    You touch down, unclip and de-kit, and Rich previews some photos with you. Your full media package follows later the same day.

Who can skydive in Devon?

You need to be at least 16, with written parental or guardian consent if you are under 18. There is no upper age limit provided you meet the medical fitness requirements. The weight limit is approximately 90kg (around 14 stone) fully clothed including shoes.

Everyone completes the British Skydiving medical declaration. If you can sign the self-declaration (Form 115a) you can do that yourself; if you have a listed medical condition or past injury you will need Form 115b signed and stamped by your doctor before you jump. Glasses and contact lenses are fine, just tell Rich beforehand.

Wear comfortable layers, because it is cooler at altitude, and flat supportive trainers or lace-up sports shoes. No boots with hooks, sandals or high heels. You are supplied with a jumpsuit, harness and goggles.

Is tandem skydiving safe?

Tandem equipment is maintained to British Skydiving standards, and every rig has a main parachute, an independent reserve parachute and an Automatic Activation Device (AAD), a computerised backup that deploys the reserve automatically if needed. Rich has been skydiving since 2012 and has made over 3,500 jumps and more than 56 hours of freefall. If you are nervous, that is normal: the jump is one-to-one, so you set the pace. Skydive Devon FAQs

Where can I skydive in Devon?

All Tailored Skydive jumps take place at Dunkeswell Airfield in Devon. Rich Taylor takes you from your first message through to the post-jump debrief, so you deal with one person throughout.

What is the difference between a 10,000ft and a 15,000ft tandem skydive?

Height. From 10,000ft you get roughly 30 seconds of freefall, and from 15,000ft up to about 60 seconds. The climb to altitude takes around 15 to 20 minutes either way. If you are torn between going higher and buying video, Rich's advice is to go higher.

How long does the freefall last, and what happens after?

Freefall is roughly 30 seconds from 10,000ft and up to about 60 seconds from 15,000ft. Your parachute opens by 5,000ft, and the noise of freefall turns into a quiet glide where you can chat and take in the view before landing.

How old do you have to be to skydive in Devon?

You must be at least 16. Anyone under 18 needs written parental or guardian consent. There is no upper age limit as long as you meet the medical fitness requirements, and there is a weight limit of approximately 90kg (around 14 stone) fully clothed.

Can I do a tandem skydive if I am scared of heights?

Yes. Nerves are completely normal, and a one-to-one jump means the pace is set by you. Rich talks through every step and every sensation before it happens, and there is a calm, reassuring jump style for anyone who wants extra time.
